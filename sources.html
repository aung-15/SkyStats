Annotated Bibliography

Data & Sources

The primary dataset behind SkyStats, plus the secondary literature each team member drew on to interpret it. Each entry below is annotated with how it informed our narrative, data critique, or visualizations.

Primary Dataset

U.S. Bureau of Transportation Statistics. T-100 Segment (All Carriers), Form 41 Traffic Data Bank. Federal Aviation Administration. transtats.bts.gov.

Our sole primary source. Legally mandated carrier reporting of every nonstop domestic and international flight segment, 2019–2025, gave us passengers, departures, seats, and distance for the ten metro areas at the center of this project. See our Data Critique for a full account of what this dataset can and cannot tell us.

Secondary Sources

Gao, Yi. 2022. "Benchmarking the recovery of air travel demands for US airports during the COVID-19 Pandemic." Transportation Research Interdisciplinary Perspectives 13: 100570. doi.org/10.1016/j.trip.2022.100570

Clusters 118 U.S. hub airports by their pandemic recovery trajectories and tests how geography shaped recovery speed. We leaned on this finding directly to interpret why our recovery map shows a Sun Belt advantage rather than a uniform national bounce-back.

Higgins-Desbiolles, Freya. 2022. "Subsidiarity in tourism and travel circuits in the face of climate crisis." Current Issues in Tourism 26 (19): 3091–3101. doi.org/10.1080/13683500.2022.2116306

Argues the pandemic sparked new appreciation for regional and domestic tourism over long-haul travel. We used this argument to explain patterns like Honolulu's appearance on our busiest-routes map only after 2020.

Javadinasr, Mohammad, Tassio Maggasy, Mahmoud Mohammadi, Kouros Mohammadain, Ehsan Rahimi, Deborah Salon, and Sybil Derrible. 2022. "The long-term effects of COVID-19 on travel behavior in the United States: A panel study on work from home, mode choice, online shopping, and air travel." Transportation Research Part F: Traffic Psychology and Behaviour 90: 466–484. doi.org/10.1016/j.trf.2022.09.019

A national panel survey finding that 48% of respondents expected permanent work-from-home flexibility and 41% of pre-pandemic business travelers expected to fly less. This is our main evidence for why business-travel-heavy metros lagged in our recovery data.

Zhang, Linfeng, Hangjun Yang, Kun Wang, Lei Bian, and Xian Zhang. "The impact of COVID-19 on airline passenger travel behavior: An exploratory analysis on the Chinese aviation market." Journal of Air Transport Management 95 (2021): 102084. doi.org/10.1016/j.jairtraman.2021.102084

An international comparison point: examines how Chinese passengers' booking and risk behavior shifted during COVID-19, useful for gauging whether patterns we found in U.S. data were globally distinctive or part of a worldwide behavioral shift.

Lamb, Tracy L., Keith J. Ruskin, Stephen Rice, Leili Khorassani, Scott R. Winter, and Dothang Truong. "A qualitative analysis of social and emotional perspectives of airline passengers during the COVID-19 pandemic." Journal of Air Transport Management 94 (2021): 102079. doi.org/10.1016/j.jairtraman.2021.102079

Gives voice to the emotional dimension of pandemic-era flying that our T-100 dataset structurally cannot capture — anxiety, risk perception, and decision-making — grounding our data critique's argument that passengers are reduced to numbers.

Sotomayor-Castillo, Cristina, Kaitlyn Radford, Cecilia Li, Shizar Nahidi, and Ramon Z. Shaban. "Air travel in a COVID-19 world: Commercial airline passengers' health concerns and attitudes towards infection prevention and disease control measures." Infection, Disease & Health 26, no. 2 (2021): 110–117. doi.org/10.1016/j.idh.2020.11.002

Surveys passenger health concerns and attitudes toward infection control, another humanistic counterpoint to the purely operational T-100 records, used in framing our "passengers as numbers" critique.

Jafari, Nahid. 2022. "The chaos on US domestic airline passenger demand forecasting caused by COVID-19." International Journal of Business Forecasting and Marketing Intelligence 7 (3): 241–258. doi.org/10.1504/IJBFMI.2022.122901

Documents how classical statistical forecasting models broke down under pandemic-era volatility. We cite this in our literature review to explain why a fresh, humanities-informed look at the T-100 data was worthwhile.

Garrow, Laurie A., Virginie Lurkin, and Lavanya Marla. 2022. "Airline order innovations soar during COVID-19 recovery." Operations Research Forum 3 (1): Article 14. doi.org/10.1007/s43069-022-00131-1

Covers how airlines innovated ancillary and merchandising strategy during recovery, background for our discussion of why airlines concentrated capacity on fewer, fuller flights rather than simply restoring pre-pandemic schedules.

Dey Tirtha, Sudipta, Tanmoy Bhowmik, and Naveen Eluru. 2022. "An airport level framework for examining the impact of COVID-19 on airline demand." Transportation Research Part A: Policy and Practice 159: 169–181. doi.org/10.1016/j.tra.2022.03.014

Models airport-level demand shocks and recovery, methodologically close to our own MSA-level approach and a useful check on our choice to analyze recovery metro-by-metro rather than only nationally.

Bielecki, Michel, Dipti Patel, Jochen Hinkelbein, et al. "Air travel and COVID-19 prevention in the pandemic and peri-pandemic period: A narrative review." Travel Medicine and Infectious Disease 39 (2021): 101915. doi.org/10.1016/j.tmaid.2020.101915

A narrative review of the public-health measures airlines and airports adopted, useful background for our timeline's public health and travel-restriction milestones.

Bao, Xiaoge, Peng Ji, Wei Lin, Matjaž Perc, and Jürgen Kurths. "The impact of COVID-19 on the worldwide air transportation network." Royal Society Open Science 8, no. 11 (2021): 210682. doi.org/10.1098/rsos.210682

A network-science analysis of how COVID-19 reshaped global air route networks, supporting our own route-restoration versus passenger-traffic distinction on the Narrative page.

Lau, Hien, Veria Khosrawipour, Piotr Kocbach, et al. "The association between international and domestic air traffic and the coronavirus (COVID-19) outbreak." Journal of Microbiology, Immunology and Infection 53, no. 3 (2020): 467–472. doi.org/10.1016/j.jmii.2020.03.026

An early-pandemic epidemiological study linking air traffic patterns to outbreak spread, useful context for why international travel collapsed faster and recovered more slowly than domestic travel in our data.

Hotle, Susan, and Stacey Mumbower. "The impact of COVID-19 on domestic U.S. air travel operations and commercial airport service." Transportation Research Interdisciplinary Perspectives 9 (2021): 100277. doi.org/10.1016/j.trip.2020.100277

Empirical grounding for domestic-specific operational effects (reduced service, schedule cuts) that parallels our own departures and distance findings for 2019–2023.

Sugishita, Kashin, Hiroki Mizutani, and Shinya Hanaoka. "Disruption and recovery of the US domestic airline networks during the COVID-19 pandemic." Journal of Air Transport Management 114 (2024): 102504. doi.org/10.1016/j.jairtraman.2023.102504

Directly parallels our Route Restoration Index: studies how the structure of the U.S. domestic airline network broke apart and reformed, which we build on in distinguishing "route recovery" from "traffic recovery" in our narrative.
